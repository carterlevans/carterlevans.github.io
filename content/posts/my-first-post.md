Like so many others before me, I will write the immortal words.

> I've actually been meaning to start a blog for a while now

Nobody ever starts their blog when they wanted to. You wait 2-10 years until the oppertune moment strikes. 

Well here it is. We've tried other methods, but I think this one has the most sticking power. There's a blogspot or two that carries my name out there, there's my college coffee review blog that's out there somewhere, but I think I'll have the most success with this one, for no other reason than this is the latest attempt.

I don't really want to put a theme to this blog, it will mostly be a place to act as a public journal of sorts, where I write what I'm wondering about at any given time. I make no promise of schedule, and mostly I'm writing it for me. I would like to be writing more, and to be writing my own words --- not those of generative AI.

To be clear, I don't have a real problem with the use of generative AI in the way that some do, I use it for most of the writing I do for work, and some of the posts on this blog will be talking about projects I'm doing that use AI. But, I feel like writing is like all muscles, used or lost. I'd rather not lose it, and I'd like to put these thoughts somewhere. Thus: *Ramblings* (working title).

Part of the impetus for this was going through the process of transferring over my work set up to the Macbook I am writing this on. This repo is about two years old according to GitHub, and I attempted this before with GitHub Pages with an old ThinkPad. But with a clear writing set up identical to my workstation, a new keyboard that is really growing on me it felt like it was time to try again. 

Instead of adopting a platform such as Medium or Substack, I decided that running a static site through GitHub Pages would be best for this project. I first created a Medium account, and took psychic damage as it demanded I follow at least three topics and bombarded me with an infinite scroll feed. Instantly put off, I went for a more self reliant solution. If I'd tried Substack first, you might be reading this there, but I've not tried it so maybe I would also be put off by it. Besides, as of now I have no desire to monetize this or even develop a readership, which I feel is the appeal of those types of sites.

I settled on [Zola](https://getzola.org) because it's familiar. Readers aware of my fight for a real CMS at work will have a good laugh that I am now resigning myself to using the very workflow I fought against for so long -- but somehow that guy is always right. 

Writing these as .md files in `doom-emacs` does just work, and is simple enough. I've seen org-mode and .org recommended as superior for this purpose, and [ox-zola](https://github.com/gicrisf/ox-zola) seems well reputed, and as an adventure down the line I will probably attempt it (and document the migration here). But I didn't want learning new syntax to be a barrier for me. I wanted to write.

After some fuddling, I set up this theme, [still](https://www.getzola.org/themes/still/), since I felt that it was close enough. I wanted to spend more time writing, and less time customizing a website. 

Again, I won't be settling on a certain topic or theme for this blog. In the past I feel like that's been a barrier for me --- pigeonholing myself into writing about this or that, defining a content calendar, etc etc etc. While some structure is helpful, I am writing because I want to, and so will write what I want to when I want to. This will likely be a lot of talk about baseball, but there are other stories worth preserving and writing about. 

Some future highlights include: 

- An explanation of my [Baseball Keeper](https://carterlevans.github.io/baseball-keeper) project and ranking methods, as I attempt to disentangle fame/notoriety and WAR.
- Diving down the rabbit trail of genealogy and discovered Kate's secret citizenship
- The process of backing up my father's blogspot and perhaps archiving it on its own site. 

I have a few ideas already in the works, and hope to have them up soon. 

Hopefully, to those I talk to regularly, if they stumble upon this it will be completely uninteresting to them. Mostly I'll be writing about whatever is on my mind, and so will have bored them to death by explaining it to them already. 

If you don't talk to me regularly and have stumbled upon this randomly, it will probably also be completely uninteresting. I don't have too many original thoughts or experiences.
